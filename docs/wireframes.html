Part one

The shopper's conversation

One thread, no redirects, four turns from "I need X" to a receipt. Judging is a walking format: a judge stands at the station for maybe ninety seconds, so every screen has to survive being read from a metre and a half away, over noise, by someone who has already seen six chatbots.

Four rules the surface has to obey

  • The limit is set before anything is searched. A spend cap chosen in turn one is a natural UI gesture and the Intent Mandate. Consent stops being a modal we bolt on at the end and becomes the frame of the whole conversation.
  • Money never appears without its scope. Any screen showing a price also shows what the agent is allowed to do with it. The gold band in C5 is the only gold on the page.
  • A decline is a fork, not a wall. C8 offers two ways forward. A dead end in a demo reads as a bug; a recovery reads as a product.
  • The Trust Panel is scenery until it matters. It sits quiet on the right, then earns its place the moment a link turns red.
Part two

Part three

One agent becomes six

A single model holding the whole conversation — every category pack, every product row, the cart and the card — is exactly the setup that invents a SKU, quotes last turn's price, or offers sizing advice about a pair of headphones. Splitting the work into specialists with narrow, typed jobs removes most of that surface, and the split is worth building for a second reason: it lets us say, truthfully, that no language model can move money.

Why this actually stops cross-category bleed

What the Guardian checks, on every hop

  • Schema. The message parses into the declared type, or it never leaves.
  • Grounding. Every sku mentioned exists in this merchant's catalog; every price and attribute matches the database row to the cent. One repair attempt, then a deterministic fallback — a plain table built from the rows in code.
  • Scope. Products outside the session's category or merchant scope are stripped.
  • Mandate. Nothing proposes a cart above the Intent Mandate's cap, and nothing reaches the payment executor without a cart mandate and a human confirmation.

Every check emits a TrustEvent on the existing bus, which means the Trust Panel is already wired for this — Y4's /trust/events stream needs no contract change, only more event sources.

On cost — the objection to answer before a judge raises it

More agents does not mean more spend. Each specialist runs on a short, purpose-built context instead of one ballooning transcript, so a full demo run stays under the $0.05 target in T-24. The concierge is nano-tier, discovery emits a tiny JSON object, and the two most expensive boxes in the pipeline — cart and payment — make no model calls at all.
